Welcome to my website!

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Department of Political Science and a Social and Ethical Responsibilities of Computing (SERC) Group Leader at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT). In June 2026, I received my Ph.D. in Political Science from Stockholm University.

I study the politics of Artificial Intelligence (AI), driven by the observation that the rapid development of AI poses one of the most consequential governance challenges of our time. Specifically, my research agenda consists of two parallel strands: As a Wallenberg Postdoc, I examine the interplay between states and firms over influence in AI Governance. In a second strand, building largely on my dissertation, I investigate international cooperation efforts to shape AI governance at the global and regional levels of governance.

Empirically, I draw on a range of data sources and engage in large-N data collection efforts, employing methodological approaches such as observational analysis and quantitative text analysis.

I am currently an instructor of record for a SERC scholar group at the Schwarzman College of Computing . This research-oriented class provides a framework to incorporate humanities, social science, and policy perspectives into MIT’s teaching, research, and implementation of computing.

I have been a visiting scholar at the Niehaus Center for Globalization and Governance at Princeton University, and was a Global Political Economy Project (GPEP) Affiliate Fellow at the Mortara Center for International Studies at Georgetown University and am affiliated with the Stockholm Center on Global Governance (SCGG) .
